## 1. OSDS purpose, scope and boundaries

**R§1, N§4; historical decision status: owner-requested reconstruction of an agreement, not transcript-authenticated agreement.** The supplied account describes OSDS as concerned with human-scale, ground-based physical observation systems, instrumentation, methods and sources. Large governmental or scientific infrastructures remain relevant when their observations operate at that scale; organisational size is not the proposed exclusion criterion.

The reported division places satellite, aerial and Earth-observation mapping primarily with GISDS; specialised biological instrumentation and biological evidence primarily with a future biological database. Interdisciplinary links should preserve relationships without duplicating another database's authority. These are proposed scientific responsibility boundaries to reconcile, not automatic deletion rules.

**A — scope tensions requiring review:** Does “aerial” exclude aircraft atmospheric observations, or aerial mapping specifically? How are autonomous ocean floats, underwater/subsurface systems, laboratory and industrial measurements treated? Which biological instrumentation is specialised while camera trapping, bioacoustics and beehive sensing remain relevant? How are mixed providers handled at product level? Existing IAGOS, Argo, NASA, Copernicus and GBIF records must retain their identities and history while those questions are assessed. Location metadata and use of GIS in analysis do not by themselves settle catalogue ownership.
